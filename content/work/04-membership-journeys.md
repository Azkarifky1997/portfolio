## Four places to go, *one member*

The organisation's services were spread across several places: a public website, a separate logged-in member area, a separate learning platform for studies, and a separate place to buy qualifications. It wanted to bring them together into one space, so members weren't confused moving between platforms.

+++
section: challenge
eyebrow: The challenge
question: How might we give members one place to see where they are, what comes next and how to get there?
+++

## Members didn't know *what came next*

Members weren't sure how to progress as a member, how to qualify, how to buy qualifications or how to keep learning. A lot of guidance happened by hand, with members having to contact staff. Staff were also checking eligibility by hand, even though most of the information was already in the CRM.

## What we heard from *staff and members*

- systems | Services were scattered | Members had to work out which platform to use for what.
- target | Members couldn't see their own progress | There was no single view of where they were or what came next.
- inclusive | Guidance relied on staff | Members often had to contact someone to find out how to progress.
- research | Checks were done by hand | Staff checked eligibility by hand, even though the data was already in the CRM.
- measure | Work was repeated | Some work was repeated across departments.

+++
section: thinking
+++

We set out to automate as much as we could. Reviewing feasibility with the CRM and development teams showed that part of the automation would not fit the first release, so I designed it fully and phased it, and kept staff review for unusual cases rather than forcing everything through rules.

## From scattered platforms to *one service*

- Services spread across several platforms | One joined-up site with a single member portal
- Members unsure how to progress | A portal where members track their CPD, qualifications and studies in one place, personalised to them
- Guidance given by hand | Routine guidance and eligibility checks built into the service, with staff kept for unusual cases
- Members lapsing | Renewal and lapse reminders
- No clear view of how the service performs | The portal lets the organisation track its KPIs
- Too much for one release | Phased delivery, with automated exemptions fully designed for a later phase

## What I'd do *differently*

- **Journeys take the shape of the business.** In large organisations, journeys quietly start to follow who owns which step, which team holds which record and what the system can do. Members were the ones stitching it all together.
- **Keep bringing it back to the member.** In every workshop I asked what a step would feel like from the member's side. The organisation's structure should stay behind the scenes.
- **One place is only better if it's simpler.** Joining platforms together meant designing the paths between them, not just moving pages.
