## Moving member services *online*

The organisation wanted to move manual member services online and rebuild how members join, progress and renew, so the website does the routine work and staff handle the cases that need a person.

![A simple service blueprint with three lanes: member, system and staff. The member applies or renews online. The system checks eligibility from existing records; a straightforward case is approved automatically. An unusual case goes to staff, who review exceptions only.](member-services-blueprint.svg "Who does what in the redesigned service. Recreated and anonymised.")

+++
section: challenge
eyebrow: The challenge
question: How might members join, progress and renew without staff checking every case by hand?

## Manual work, and *guesswork* for members

Members moved between several membership levels, and many steps relied on manual work by staff and guesswork by members. Checking whether someone was eligible meant staff cross-referencing records by hand, even though much of that information already sat in the CRM. Members were not always sure which level was right for them, and the organisation wanted renewals to run smoothly so fewer people lapsed.

## Working in the open with *every team*

The answers sat across several teams and systems, so I worked in the open with all of them. I mapped each department's processes in shared working documents they could review and correct, and checked with CRM operators and developers what the systems could and could not do.

## What we heard from *staff and members*

- systems | Nobody had the whole picture | Every department did things differently.
- research | Checks were done by hand | Staff were checking eligibility by hand, even though most of the information was already in the CRM.
- inclusive | Members weren't sure where they fitted | Members weren't always sure which level was right for them.
- measure | Work was repeated | Some work was repetitive or duplicated.

+++
section: thinking
+++

We set out to automate as much as we could. Reviewing feasibility with the CRM and development teams showed that part of the automation would not fit the first release, so I designed it fully and phased it, and kept staff review for unusual cases rather than forcing everything through rules.

+++

eyebrow: The solution

## From insight to *design decision*

- Eligibility checked by hand | Worked out from records already held
- Every case reviewed by staff | Routine cases automatic, unusual ones kept for staff
- Members lapsing | Renewal and lapse reminders
- Too much for one release | Phased delivery, with automated exemptions fully designed for a later phase

## What I'd do *differently*

- **Journeys take the shape of the business.** In large organisations, journeys quietly start to follow who owns which step, which team holds which record and what the system can do.
- **Members do the stitching.** Every decision made sense to the team making it, but members were the ones stitching it all together.
- **Keep bringing it back to the member.** In every workshop I asked what a step would feel like from the member's side. The organisation's structure should stay behind the scenes.
