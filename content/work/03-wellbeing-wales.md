## A neutral answer to a *simple question*

WE Cymru is the Wales hub of the global Wellbeing Economy Alliance. It wanted a neutral answer to a simple question: is the population at large, not a passionate minority, ready for a transition to an economy that serves people and place?

## Reaching *everyone*, not just the people who already care

## A survey for the whole country, *dialogues* for the rest

### Choice grids
People pinned themselves on a grid to show which statement they were closest to, then added a note saying why. That gave us numbers and reasons in one question.

### Going to where people are
Roundtables in communities, and one-to-one interviews in rural Wales, filmed with a filmmaker.

### Including the youngest voice
One dialogue included a seven-year-old, with a parent alongside. We had consent from both the child and the parent, including for recording.

### Three kinds of awareness
We segmented awareness into "I've heard of it", "I'm familiar with it" and "I believe in it". The 2,000 written responses were analysed by hand and with AI, and checked against the raw data.

## What we heard from *Wales*

- workshop | Awareness changes with age | Almost **twice as many** 18 to 34-year-olds were aware of the wellbeing economy as people aged 65 and over.
- heart | People expect it to be better for them | Asked about 15 areas of daily life, people most often expected things to get better.
- research | Healthcare, housing and education | In unprompted written answers, these were the three most important areas a wellbeing economy would improve.
- book | Education shapes the choice | **4 in 10** people educated to GCSE level or below said they needed more support and information to choose.
- globe | Local reasons differ | In the Vale of Glamorgan, people chose it for wellbeing and quality of life (**25%**), fairness and social justice (**20%**), economic sustainability (**14%**) and ethical and political reasons (**9%**).

The findings fed into WE Cymru's first Wellbeing Economy Cymru conference (November 2024, over 500 delegates), were shared at Davos by the agency's CEO, and became the evidence for a pilot proposal in the Vale of Glamorgan. WE Cymru describes its research as reaching over 800 people across Wales.

## What I'd do *differently*

- **Pick the method for the person.** A survey reached the whole country, but the dialogues reached people a survey would miss.
- **Plan for the youngest and oldest voices from the start.**
- **Agree what happens next.** The persona stories stayed at draft stage, so next time I'd agree up front how the findings will be used.
