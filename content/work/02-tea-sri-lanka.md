## The challenge

Sri Lanka has over 450,000 smallholder tea farmers, and tea is the country's biggest agricultural export. Yet many smallholders live with almost no financial safety net.

In 2020, Dialog Axiata, Sri Lanka's largest mobile operator, received a grant from the GSMA Innovation Fund to test digital services that could improve smallholder farmers' financial inclusion and climate resilience. Weather-index insurance was one of the ideas being explored.

Tea factories already lend money to farmers, and farmers already repay them. Our question was whether that existing, trusted relationship could also carry insurance, and whether farmers would actually adopt it.

## Fieldwork

My two colleagues and I went to smallholder tea farms in Sri Lanka's Southern Province to see the whole system first-hand.

- **Sitting with farmers.** We interviewed farmers in Sinhala, so they could speak freely in their own language. We talked about their living standards, how weather affected their income month by month, how they used loans from the factory and how they repaid them.

![](Farmer interviews.jpeg "Sitting with farmers during interviews")

- **Riding with leaf collectors.** We rode along with the collectors who visit each farmer, weigh their bagged tea leaves and put a price on them. This showed us a regular, face-to-face link between farmers and the factory.

![](Leaf collector weighing tea leaves.jpeg "A leaf collector weighing bagged tea leaves")

- **Talking to factory staff.** We spoke with factory staff about how they buy tea from smallholders and how their lending works.
- **Synthesis.** Afterwards, we synthesised everything on the wall, clustering what we heard into themes, pain points and opportunities, then mapped farmers' journeys across the year.

![](Synthesis walls .jpeg "Synthesising the fieldwork")

## What we learned

- **Drought can wipe out income for months.** In dry seasons, farmers' whole fields could be burnt, leaving them with no income for months at a time. The need for protection was real and urgent.
- **Insurance appealed, but wasn't understood.** Farmers liked the idea of insurance, but at first many did not understand how it worked.
- **Farmers were wary of being sold to.** Many were scared of being sold yet another product. Trust mattered as much as the product itself.
- **One phone per household, and not a smartphone.** Usually a whole household shared one basic phone. An app-only service would have excluded most of the people it was meant for.
- **The leaf collector is a trusted, regular visitor.** Collectors already visit each farmer to weigh and price their tea, making them a familiar point of contact with the factory.

> "I've heard of mobile money, but I'm not really sure what use it is to me..."
>
> An elderly tea farmer, quoted by GSMA from a fact-finding visit in the Southern tea district

## What we delivered

We handed over a research report, journey maps showing farmers' lives and finances across the year, and recommendations on how a digital finance and insurance service could fit those lives.

## What happened next

The wider programme continued after our research. GSMA reported in 2021 that Dialog had integrated the ability for tea factories to pay smallholder farmers through its eZ Cash mobile money system, and had developed a mobile money model for farmers to request payment advances from factories. Dialog was also exploring crop-specific weather-index insurance and agricultural loans. These steps belong to the whole programme, not to our research alone.

## Reflection

This was the project that pulled me into design, and its lessons still shape how I work.

- **Start with people, not technology.** We arrived thinking about digital services and met households sharing one basic phone. The right question was never "what can the technology do?" but "how do these people actually live?"
- **Trust is part of the product.** Farmers wanted protection but feared being sold to. A financial product for people like this has to be explained clearly, delivered through people they already trust, and honest about what it does.
- **Follow the whole system.** Riding with the leaf collectors showed us how money really moves between farmers and the factory. I still look for the people and processes behind a service, not just the customer in front of it.
- **Speak their language, literally.** Running interviews in Sinhala meant farmers could tell us what they really thought, not what they thought we wanted to hear.
