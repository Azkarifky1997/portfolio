## A safety net for *tea farmers*

Sri Lanka has over 450,000 smallholder tea farmers, and tea is the country's biggest agricultural export. Yet many smallholders live with almost no financial safety net.

In 2020, Dialog Axiata, Sri Lanka's largest mobile operator, received a grant from the GSMA Innovation Fund to test digital services that could improve smallholder farmers' financial inclusion and climate resilience. Weather-index insurance was one of the ideas being explored.

## Could a trusted channel carry *insurance*?

Tea factories already lend money to farmers, and farmers already repay them. Our question was whether that existing, trusted relationship could also carry insurance, and whether farmers would actually adopt it.

## Seeing the *whole system* first-hand

My two colleagues and I went to smallholder tea farms in Sri Lanka's Southern Province to see the whole system first-hand.

### Sitting with farmers
image: Farmer interviews.jpeg | Two researchers sitting with an elderly tea farmer on the veranda of his home, taking notes as they talk with him | Sitting with farmers during interviews
We interviewed farmers in Sinhala, so they could speak freely in their own language, about their living standards, how weather affected their income month by month, and how they used and repaid loans from the factory.

### Riding with leaf collectors
image: Leaf collector weighing tea leaves.jpeg | A tea leaf collector weighing a large mesh sack of freshly picked tea leaves on a hanging digital scale, with a woman standing beside him | A leaf collector weighing bagged tea leaves
We rode along with the collectors who visit each farmer, weigh their bagged tea leaves and put a price on them. This showed us a regular, face-to-face link between farmers and the factory.

### Synthesis on the wall
image: Synthesis walls .jpeg | A wall of green and pink sticky notes titled Key users and issues, grouped into columns under the headings Farmer and Factory | Synthesising the fieldwork
We also spoke with factory staff about how lending works. Afterwards, we clustered what we heard into themes, pain points and opportunities, then mapped farmers' journeys across the year.

## What we heard from *farmers*

- calendar | Drought can wipe out income for months | In dry seasons, whole fields could be burnt, leaving farmers with no income for months at a time.
- research | Insurance appealed, but wasn't understood | Farmers liked the idea of insurance, but at first many did not understand how it worked.
- alert | Farmers were wary of being sold to | Many were scared of being sold yet another product. Trust mattered as much as the product itself.
- phone | One phone per household, and not a smartphone | An app-only service would have excluded most of the people it was meant for.
- workshop | The leaf collector is a trusted, regular visitor | Collectors already visit each farmer to weigh and price their tea, a familiar point of contact with the factory.

> "I've heard of mobile money, but I'm not really sure what use it is to me..."
>

We set out to understand whether farmers would take up weather-index insurance. Riding with leaf collectors and sitting with farmers widened the question: most households shared one basic phone, and many farmers were wary of being sold yet another product. Trust and how the service would be explained and delivered became as important in our thinking as the insurance itself.

## What happened next

We handed over a research report, journey maps showing farmers' lives and finances across the year, and recommendations on how a digital finance and insurance service could fit those lives.

The wider programme continued after our research. GSMA reported in 2021 that Dialog had integrated the ability for tea factories to pay smallholder farmers through its eZ Cash mobile money system, and had developed a mobile money model for farmers to request payment advances from factories. Dialog was also exploring crop-specific weather-index insurance and agricultural loans.

## What I'd do *differently*

This was the project that pulled me into design, and its lessons still shape how I work.

- **Start with people, not technology.** We arrived thinking about digital services and met households sharing one basic phone. The right question was never "what can the technology do?" but "how do these people actually live?"
- **Trust is part of the product.** Farmers wanted protection but feared being sold to. A financial product for people like this has to be explained clearly, delivered through people they already trust, and honest about what it does.
- **Follow the whole system.** Riding with the leaf collectors showed us how money really moves between farmers and the factory. I still look for the people and processes behind a service.
- **Speak their language, literally.** Running interviews in Sinhala meant farmers could tell us what they really thought, not what they thought we wanted to hear.
