## The challenge

Members moved between several membership levels, and many steps relied on manual work by staff and guesswork by members. Checking whether someone was eligible meant staff cross-referencing records by hand, even though much of that information already sat in the CRM. Members were not always sure which level was right for them, and the organisation wanted renewals to run smoothly so fewer people lapsed.

## Approach

The answers sat across several teams and systems, so I worked in the open with all of them. I ran workshops and interviews with each department, mapped their processes in shared working documents they could review and correct, and checked with CRM operators and developers what the systems could and could not do. From there I designed end-to-end flows covering what the member sees and what happens behind the screen, then wireframes and functional specifications the developers could build from.

## How our thinking changed

We set out to automate as much as we could. Reviewing feasibility with the CRM and development teams showed that part of the automation would not fit the first release, so I designed it fully and phased it, and kept staff review for unusual cases rather than forcing everything through rules.

## Key design decisions

### Let the system do the checking

I designed eligibility to be worked out from records the organisation already held, instead of asking members to work it out themselves. Part of this automation was scheduled for a later phase, fully designed and ready to build.

### Automate the routine, keep people for the exceptions

Straightforward cases are handled automatically, while unusual ones still go to a staff member, so the service stays safe without staff reviewing everything.

## Reflection

In large organisations, journeys quietly start to take the shape of the business: who owns which step, which team holds which record, what the system can do. Every decision made sense to the team making it, but members were the ones stitching it all together. The most useful thing I did on this project was keep bringing the conversation back to them, asking in every workshop what a step would feel like from the member's side. It's a habit I now take into every project: the organisation's structure should stay behind the scenes, and the person using the service should never have to understand it.
